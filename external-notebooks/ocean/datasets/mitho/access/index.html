MiTHo: access and interpret Cumulative Hazard Indices

Explore re-projected dataset in the Med cube

This dataset provides monthly information on Cumulative Hazard Indexs (CHIs) at the sea surface (3D, lat, lon, time) at global scale. The dataset is split into 3 zarr stores:

  1. Global CHI1 and CHI3 indicies

  2. Global CHI2 indicies

  3. Global CHI4 indicies

    • EO4SOCIETY Website: https://eo4society.esa.int/projects/mitho/

    • Original Data Source: https://zenodo.org/records/17191460

    • Reference: https://opensciencedata.esa.int/products/global-cumulative-hazard-indexes-chis/collection.json

    • OSC entry: https://opensciencedata.esa.int/products/global-cumulative-hazard-indexes-chis/collection.json

    • License: CC-BY-4.0

This tutorial accesses the published Zarr stores over HTTPS and illustrates the multistressor construction of CHI1, global July 2022 conditions, and annual changes across the Mediterranean and four subbasins: Western Mediterranean, Adriatic, Ionian, and Levantine–Aegean. The monthly map shows July 2022; time series cover the full record.

The Mediterranean is used here as a regional example to illustrate basin-scale and subbasin-scale analyses. A global map provides context; basin analyses are examples, not a geographic restriction of the data.

Source: original NetCDF dataset on Zenodo · catalogue entry · MiTHo

IndexInputs / stressorsAvailable periodLevels
CHI1Sea surface temperature (SST): warm and cold extremes; hydrogen ion concentration (H+): acidificationJan 1993–Dec 2022Surface
CHI2Temperature: warm and cold extremes; dissolved oxygen: deoxygenation; salinity: low and high extremesJan 2004–Nov 20222.5 dbar (level=0), 200 dbar (level=1)
CHI3SST, H+, sea surface salinity (SSS), sea surface density (SSD), wind speed: nine stressors in totalJan 1993–Dec 2022Surface

The dataset is gridded on a 1° x 1° regular lat-lon grid.
Geographic coverage and valid-data masks differ between indices. CHI3 combines both low and high extremes of SSS, SSD and wind in a single index. For any given variable and threshold pair, low and high extremes are mutually exclusive at the same place and time. Extremes of different variables can occur together.

3. What does CHI1 represent?¶

The original processing code constructs the unnormalized CHI1 as:

CHI1 = S_MHW + S_CS + S_PH

S_MHW is the marine-heatwave contribution, S_CS the cold-spell contribution, and S_PH the acidification contribution. They are non-negative, dimensionless stressor intensities derived from standardized anomalies at detected extremes. They are not raw SST or pH measurements. For SST, the processing uses monthly 90th/10th percentile thresholds for warm/cold extremes, respectively.

The example uses the unnormalized, fixed-climatology variables. _mav identifies the alternative based on a moving climatology, rather than simply a smoothed CHI time series. _norm, _mean, _std, _Cat and _PI identify other derived products; they are not interchangeable with the variables plotted here.

Higher CHI values indicate a larger sum of stressor contributions under this method; they are not calibrated probabilities of ecological damage. The baseline depends on the input product and must be considered when comparing different indices.

For CHI1, _norm divides the raw index by the global spatial maximum of each month. The global context below uses raw CHI1, not this normalized variant.

8. Basin-wide annual evolution: are recent extremes stronger or more frequent?¶

Keep the same spatial sample in every month: retain basin grid cells with finite CHI1, normalized CHI1 and all three contributions for all 360 months. Use weights proportional to cos(latitude) for the equal-angle grid. The coverage table reports the retained fraction of each geographic mask; results describe those observed cells, not an extrapolation to missing parts of the basin.

Three metrics separate different aspects:

  1. Mean CHI1: mean over retained basin cells and all months of each year, including zeros.

  2. Affected area: annual mean of the monthly area fraction satisfying CHI1_norm > 1e-4.

  3. Intensity when active: annual weighted mean raw CHI1 across the active cell-months only.

The last metric helps separate magnitude from occurrence. An increase in mean CHI1 alone does not establish that individual extremes became stronger. These are descriptive comparisons under a fixed-climatology index, not a trend-significance or attribution test.

# Aggregate the monthly data by calendar year; active intensity excludes inactive cell-months.
annual_rows, decade_rows = [], []
basin_monthly = {}
for name, mask in basin_masks.items():
    w = basin_weights[name]
    retained = w > 0
    total_w = w.sum()
    field = regional.CH1_CHI1.where(retained)
    active = (regional.CH1_CHI1_norm > 1e-4) & retained
    monthly_mean = field.weighted(w).mean(("latitude", "longitude"))
    active_weight = w.where(active, 0).sum(("latitude", "longitude"))
    active_sum = (field.where(active, 0) * w).sum(("latitude", "longitude"))
    monthly_fraction = 100 * active_weight / total_w
    annual_mean = monthly_mean.resample(time="YS").mean()
    annual_area = monthly_fraction.resample(time="YS").mean()
    annual_intensity = (
        active_sum.resample(time="YS").sum() / active_weight.resample(time="YS").sum()
    )
    basin_monthly[name] = {
        "mean": monthly_mean,
        "area": monthly_fraction,
        "active_sum": active_sum,
        "active_weight": active_weight,
    }
    for i, time in enumerate(annual_mean.time.values):
        annual_rows.append(
            {
                "Basin": name,
                "Year": pd.Timestamp(time).year,
                "Mean CHI1": float(annual_mean[i]),
                "Affected area (%)": float(annual_area[i]),
                "Intensity when active": float(annual_intensity[i]),
            }
        )
    for label, start, end in [
        ("1993–2002", "1993", "2002"),
        ("2013–2022", "2013", "2022"),
    ]:
        s = slice(start, end)
        decade_rows.append(
            {
                "Basin": name,
                "Period": label,
                "Mean CHI1": float(monthly_mean.sel(time=s).mean()),
                "Affected area (%)": float(monthly_fraction.sel(time=s).mean()),
                "Intensity when active": float(
                    active_sum.sel(time=s).sum() / active_weight.sel(time=s).sum()
                ),

10. Mediterranean Persistency and Category maps¶

Use the stored products, and first verify them against the monthly normalized data. All maps cover the full 1993–2022 period:

  • Persistency: number of months with CHI1_norm > 1e-4.

  • Low Category: number of months with 0 < CHI1_norm <= 0.33.

  • Medium Category: number of months with 0.33 < CHI1_norm <= 0.66.

  • High Category: number of months with 0.66 < CHI1_norm <= 1.001.

The 1.001 upper bound is the numerical margin used in the source code; the nominal normalized upper limit is 1. Category is relative to each month’s global spatial maximum, not an absolute hazard threshold. Categories partition positive normalized values; their total can differ from PI because PI excludes values at or below 1e-4.

These full-period counts locate repeated occurrence but do not themselves show whether events increased towards the end of the record. Use the annual metrics above for that question. Maps below retain the same complete-data Mediterranean mask. Each panel has its own colour scale in months, to make the rarer high category visible; compare the colourbar values, not colours alone, between panels.
